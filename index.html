A bit about me

The interesting problems are rarely about the model itself.

They're about what happens around it: who's allowed to use it, what it's allowed to see, and how you prove any of it afterwards. That's the part I like.

I started out building machine learning models for power plants and retailers. These days I work on the infrastructure underneath AI products — the gateways, the safety checks, and the monitoring that tells you what a model actually did.
